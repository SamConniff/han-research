## 1. The brief

Hard As Nails: men paint their nails for a month as a public act of bravery. 2025 pilot — 422 signed up, 155 completed full mixed-methods assessment, 51 in the longitudinal study (post, six weeks, nine months), 50 controls. Informal academic input from Dr Stephen Burrell, Durham. Line: **"Brave Males. Great Nails. Hard Data."**

Partners Now is the partner-facing product. Baseline functionality shipped; this work is the creative layer over it.

Positioning: *do something brave that challenges cultural norms, to put yourself out there and help others.* The reframe that makes it land with men — the patriarchy is the system that holds **them** back too; restrictive stereotypes damage men first, then the relationships around them.

Sam's starting instincts: black / pink / white are the punk colours; pink into neon takes the most identifiable feminine colour and makes it masculine. Not nail salons, not camp, not vintage retro. Slightly Blitz Kids, early Depeche Mode, New Order. Eighties into nineties, a bit neon, a bit collage. Not full New Romantic, not early Adam Ant.

## 2. Verdict on the direction

**Insight — holds.** Punk's grammar is refusing an assigned role. Painting your nails is a small, cheap, public refusal. The aesthetic isn't decoration on the strategy; it's the same argument in another medium.

**Audience — holds, strongly.** The recurring participant line — *"I used to paint my nails when I was a punk / a goth / a new romantic"* — is a route back in. A 52-year-old watch manager didn't lose the capacity for self-decoration, he was told to put it away. The 1978–1990 sleeve language is proof he once had permission, and it belongs to him, not to a salon.

**Opportunity — holds, with a condition.** The badge is the opportunity; Emma proved it by asking to wear the mark before anyone asked her to. Condition: a badge worn at work must survive being seen by a boss, a client and a kid.

## 3. THE CENTRAL DECISION — the gesture rule

Danielly said the middle finger is too aggressive. He's right about the symptom, wrong about the cause, which makes his fix (soften it) wrong.

> **Aggression is not in the gesture. It is in the aim.**
> A finger down the lens is aimed at the viewer — *fuck you* to a man who has done nothing but look.
> The same hand raised off-axis, thrown at something outside the crop, reads as *fuck this* — at the rule, not the reader.
> **Every gesture points at the system, never at the audience.** One line in every brief and call sheet.

This lets Danielly and Emma both be right.

**The split:**

*The middle finger stays in the wordmark and nowhere else.* It's earned and predates all this. Brand level only — never in participant photography, never on a partner deck. Scarcity keeps it a mark rather than a mannerism. Sam's email signature (gentleman illustration flipping the bird) stays exactly as it is: it works because it's singular. It is not a system and must not be asked to become one.

*The two-fingered V is the people's gesture.* Participant portraits, email signatures, badges, stickers, partner pack. Specifically British, mischievous rather than furious. Legible at 40px in a grid where a single finger reads as a smudge. Shows twice as many nails. Emma can have it immediately.

**The evidence Sam assembled** (now a gallery in the board): Churchill 1942, Kes, Vyvyan, Rik Mayall, Jarvis Cocker, Liam and Noel Gallagher, Oasis, Steve McQueen ×2, Robbie Williams. Seven decades — Churchill made it victory, Kes made it working-class and English, punk made it rude, Britpop made it a badge. Every one is aimed off-frame. The rule isn't an invention; it's what everyone who uses the gesture well already does.

## 4. Palette — two colours, one attitude

The discipline is the constraint, not the colours. Punk sleeves were two-colour because two-colour was cheap: Malcolm Garrett reduced Linder Sterling's full-colour montage for *Orgasm Addict* to two colours purely to cut the print bill. Adopt the constraint and the aesthetic comes free.

| Role | Hex | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | `#0A0709` | Warm near-black, not `#000`. Never a white page with black type. |
| Rhodamine | `#E6007E` | Already live on the research site. Process magenta — the ink, not a tint. Full strength or not at all; a 40% pink is a nursery. |
| Hot | `#FF338F` | Screen-only lift for links and states. Keep out of artwork briefs. |
| Paper | `#F3ECEF` | Off-white with a pink bias, so whites look printed not lit. Pure `#FFF` only in the wordmark. |
| Smiley | `#E4FF3D` | Third ink, rationed. A participant described his own nails as "neon pink and 'smiley' yellow" — the audience handed us the acid-house accent unprompted. Alerts, deadlines, campaign month only. |

Typography on the board: Archivo (expanded heavy grotesque, display), Newsreader (body serif), JetBrains Mono (catalogue metadata). All Google Fonts.

## 5. The hundred men

The existing photographs aren't twee because of the nails. They're twee because of soft light, white backgrounds, apologetic smiles, and hands held out for inspection like a swatch. That is salon grammar, doing the opposite of what the research says.

**No reshoot — treat what exists.**
1. Duotone: black and rhodamine, two plates, no third colour, no greys that aren't made of dots.
2. Coarse, visibly dotted halftone. Grain kills preciousness faster than art direction.
3. Crop tight and hard — hands and faces only. Kitchens, sofas and salon interiors are where the tweeness lives.
4. Grid them. One man is a portrait; a hundred in a 10 × 10 wall is a movement.
5. Keep WhatsApp screengrabs untreated as counterpoint — that's the evidence layer.

**First action: treat twenty frames.** A day's work; answers rescue-or-reshoot before committing budget.

**2026 shoot brief.**
1. Hard direct flash, black ground, low horizon. Shoot a fireman like a monument, not a client (ref: Brian Griffin, *A Broken Frame*).
2. No smiling for approval. Deadpan or defiant.
3. Hands as fists, Vs and grips — never displayed palm-down.
4. Shoot in the uniform, the hi-vis, the kit. Workwear colliding with painted nails is the whole story in one frame.
5. Every gesture aimed off-frame.

Note: participant portraits by elle@bornormade.com (Shane L. Lloyd, Mark Horne, Anthony Cornfield, Jan Keck, Alan DeBruyne, Marc Tobias, Dafydd Griffiths) sit loose in `005_Creative_Brand` at 2–6MB each.

## 6. Reference board — credits verified

Verified against MoMA, the V&A, Art of ZTT, Factory Records and Discogs.

**Lane A — photomontage as argument**
- **Buzzcocks, *Orgasm Addict*** (New Hormones, 1977). Montage: Linder Sterling. Design: Malcolm Garrett. Iron-headed nude spliced from pornography and homes-and-gardens magazines; reduced to two colours to save money; held by MoMA. *Take:* the method. A feminist photomontage about gendered bodies, on a punk sleeve, in black and one hot colour. **Reference of record.**
- **Crass, *Penis Envy*** (1981). Gee Vaucher. *Take:* the seriousness, and the stencil system — one repeatable mark everywhere.

**Lane B — the sleeve carries the data**
- **Frankie Goes To Hollywood, *Two Tribes*** (ZTT, 1984). Design XL/ZTT, photography Anton Corbijn, sleeve text Paul Morley — including a chart of the biological effects of nuclear fallout. *Take:* the exact move for HAN. MIAB scores, control group, empathy at nine months, as sleeve notes on the back of a poster rather than a PDF nobody opens.
- **Cabaret Voltaire, *Red Mecca* / *2x45* / *Micro-Phonies*** (Fetish & Some Bizzare, 1981–84). Neville Brody, art director at Fetish, then *The Face* 1981–86. Hand-made, PMT, film overlay, pre-computer. *Take:* a proprietary symbol set — ten nails, a V, a hazard mark, a tick — drawn once, used as punctuation. Cheaper than illustration, far more ownable.
- **New Order, *Blue Monday* (FAC 73)** (Factory, 1983). Peter Saville. Die-cut floppy-disk sleeve; title and catalogue number encoded as a colour code decipherable only via the wheel on the back of *Power, Corruption & Lies*. *Take:* withholding as confidence. A brand that makes you lean in reads adult, and adult is the antidote to twee.

**Lane C — neon collage, 1988–92**
- **Happy Mondays, *Pills 'n' Thrills and Bellyaches*** (Factory FACT 320, 1990). Central Station Design; first pressings collaged real US candy wrappers (Factory flew them to LA for a week), later altered for copyright. *Take:* the tone — joyful, blokey, no art-school apology. And the lesson: collage our own found material. The WhatsApp group is better because nobody can take it off us.
- **Pop Will Eat Itself, *This Is the Day… This Is the Hour… This Is This!*** (RCA, 1989). The Designers Republic, Sheffield. Pseudo-corporate marks, hazard symbols, fake official language. Held by the V&A. *Take:* parodying institutional design. A brand that mimics the safety notice gets further inside a fire station than one that looks like a charity.

**Lane D — day-glo pink at full strength**
- **X-Ray Spex, *Germfree Adolescents*** (EMI International, 1978). Sleeve Cooke Key; concept Falcon Stuart; photography Trevor Key. *Take:* the saturation level and the politics under it — day-glo as refusal, not fun.
- **Sex Pistols, *Never Mind the Bollocks*** (Virgin, 1977). Jamie Reid. *Take:* the pink-and-acid pair **only**. Leave the ransom-note lettering — most exhausted device in graphic design.

**Lane E — how to photograph an ordinary man**
- **Depeche Mode, *A Broken Frame*** (Mute, 1982). Photography Brian Griffin, design Martyn Atkins. Woman scything corn under a storm sky, in the language of Soviet socialist realism; *Life* named it one of the best photographs of the 1980s. *Take:* the whole answer to twee — ordinary labour photographed as monumental.
- **Depeche Mode, *Violator*** (Mute, 1990). Anton Corbijn. One red bloom on black. *Take:* restraint; one saturated colour on black carrying an emotional argument alone.

**Lane F — the world it lives in** (from Sam's folder)
- Derek Ridgers, Blitz 1979–80 — the club photographed straight; ordinary young Londoners dressed up and unembarrassed. Closest thing to a finished photographic brief.
- Neville Brody's *The Face*; *The Face* generally.
- Barney Bubbles ×2 (Stiff) — wit, geometry, cheap print. Funny without twee: hardest thing on the board to get right.
- Kraftwerk — uniform as statement, not hiding place.
- The Haçienda (Ben Kelly) — hazard stripes and industrial signage; a nightclub dressed as a workplace. Exactly the hi-vis/painted-nails collision.
- Grace Jones, *Nightclubbing* (1981, Jean-Paul Goude) — the answer to whether a hard image can carry a soft idea.
- Anton Corbijn set — grain and contrast as honesty; clean retouching reads as marketing.

## 7. What it isn't

- **Nail salon** — no script logotypes, dusty-rose gradients, bottle-and-brush photography, hands held out for inspection. The nail is a mark, not a manicure.
- **Camp** — warmth and humour yes; knowingness and wink no. The joke is on the rule, never on the men.
- **Vintage retro** — not pastiche of 1979. Take the method, apply it to 2026 photographs of 2026 men.
- **Full New Romantic** — no frills, ruffs or Adam Ant stripe. The men wear hi-vis.
- **Ransom note** — cut up the images, set the type straight.
- **Charity** — no soft focus, muted palette or rounded corners. We have a control group. Look like research, behave like a movement.

## 8. Three true things

- **420 BC — the middle finger is 2,400 years old.** Aristophanes, *The Clouds*, used to mock Socrates. Rome: *digitus impudicus*. Older than every stereotype we're asking men to drop.
- **1918 — pink was a boys' colour.** Earnshaw's *Infants' Department*, June 1918: "the generally accepted rule is pink for the boys, and blue for the girls… pink, being a more decided and stronger colour, is more suitable for the boy." Jo B. Paoletti, *Pink and Blue* (2012).
- **1415 — DO NOT use the Agincourt story.** The tale that English archers invented the V-sign is a myth: no chronicle records it, it only became popular in the 1990s, and the British Library finds no link. If a date is needed, the V-sign appears in the Macclesfield Psalter c.1330.

## 10. Technical constraints discovered (saves the next session rediscovering them)

- **This container's egress proxy blocks most of the web.** `upload.wikimedia.org`, `en.wikipedia.org`, `commons.wikimedia.org` all return 403. Image files cannot be downloaded here; the web-fetch tool returns text only, never bytes.
- **Pulling images through the Drive connector is prohibitively expensive.** One 26KB file cost roughly 70,000 tokens round trip. The 3MB folder would cost millions. Do not embed images as data URIs by this route.
- **Published artifacts cannot load external images.** A strict CSP blocks every outside host. Only inlined data URIs render. Hence: the artifact carries the argument, the Drive copy carries the pictures.
- Relative-path images work fine in the local/Drive copy of the HTML. That is the working solution.
